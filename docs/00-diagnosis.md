## The real problem

> He cannot track what he has delegated. He hands things off to his team / founder's
> office, is "bad at follow-ups," and is bad at remembering whose bucket something landed
> in. Things fall into a black hole until his memory randomly surfaces them — by which
> point the other person has an excuse ready.

This is an **open-loops problem, not an inbox problem.**

## What it is NOT

An earlier pass guessed the energy leak was finance-ops plumbing (fund transfers, bank
statements, payroll, RSU stamp duty, audit data). **That guess was wrong and was
explicitly rejected.** Do not rebuild around it. Finance-ops mail matters only where it
is a *hard-deadline regulatory item he personally must not miss* (System 1) or a *loop
stalled on his sign-off* (open loop) — not as a category to track for its own sake.

## What "done well" looks like to him

- Everything **tech-driven and tracked** — reminders, follow-ups, timelines.
- The **effective productivity of his three team members amplified**, not just his own.
- Not a one-off tracker but a **sustainable routine** that runs on its own, updates him
  daily/weekly, asks whether it's actually helping, and improves.
- A measurable outcome: **~30% less time on operational noise**, that time protected for
  the ₹1,000 Cr Fund II raise.
- The system **actively protects his time** — a shield, not a status report.

## Design consequences

1. The unit of value is a **closed loop**, not a processed email.
2. Every surfaced item names an **owner** and a **clock** (days elapsed / deadline).
3. Output is **short and readable** (half a page daily), delivered to his inbox.
4. Success is measured in **his time reclaimed**, and the feedback loop is how we know.
